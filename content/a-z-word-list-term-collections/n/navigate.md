## navigate, navigation

In most situations, avoid using *navigate* (verb) and *navigation* (noun or adjective) because they're formal words that might not localize well. Use simpler words instead.

**See also**  
[browse](../b/browse)  
[Describing interactions with the UI](../../procedures-instructions/describing-interactions-with-ui)

## Alternatives to navigate

| To describe | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Going from place to place on the internet or an intranet | *browse* | To browse the web, select any link on your home page. |
| Going directly to a webpage or website, whether by entering a URL or by selecting a link | *go to* | To go to a webpage, enter the address of the page on the address bar, and then select **Go**. |
| Going to a folder or file | *browse* if the user has to find it      *go to* if the action is direct | Go to your **Downloads** folder and open the file. |
| Moving through a page, file, or app | *move through* | Use the **Next** buttons to move through the pages. |

## Alternatives to navigation

Avoid referring to UI controls and buttons as *navigation buttons* and to webpages that orient the customer as *navigation topics* or *navigation pages*. Most of the time, the *navigation* part is unnecessary and you can simply use *buttons*, *topics,* and *pages*.

Don't use *navigation pane* or *navigation menu* unless the term appears in the UI. Use *left pane* or *left menu* instead if the use of a direction is appropriate.

If you need to use *navigation*, never abbreviate it to *nav* (as in *nav pane* or *left nav*) because it's slang.

**Examples**  
Use the buttons to make your selection.  
On the service menu, select your service. Then, on the left pane, select your resource.

You don't need to find an alternative to *navigation* when it's the most precise word in the context.

**Example**  
This change improves code organization and navigation.
